Understanding Pranayama

What Is Pranayama?

Pranayama is a traditional yogic practice based on conscious awareness and regulation of the breath. Different pranayama breathing exercises use patterns of inhalation, exhalation and breath control.

Pranayama is traditionally practiced alongside yoga, meditation and other wellness routines. Different techniques have different breathing patterns and should be learned with attention to comfort and technique.

Types of Pranayama & Breathing Techniques

Kapalbhati

A traditional yogic breathing practice involving active exhalations and passive inhalations.

02

Anulom Vilom

A traditional alternate-nostril breathing practice emphasizing controlled breathing and awareness.

03

Naadi Shodhan

A traditional alternate-nostril pranayama practice used within yoga and mindful breathing routines.

04

Bhastrika

A traditional breathing technique characterized by active inhalation and exhalation.

05

Ujjayi

A controlled yogic breathing technique traditionally practiced with gentle throat constriction.

06

Sheetali – Sheetkari

Traditional cooling-oriented pranayama practices commonly incorporated into yoga routines.

07

Brahmari

Close ears with both thumbs, hear inner sound and produce humming vibration.

08

Omkar

A traditional practice combining conscious breathing with vocalization of the Om sound.

Pranayama for Beginners

How to Practice Pranayama

Beginners can approach pranayama gradually by learning the purpose and breathing pattern of each technique before increasing practice time or intensity.

Practice in a comfortable position and focus on steady, controlled breathing. Avoid forcing the breath. Stop if you experience dizziness, discomfort or unusual symptoms.

The appropriate technique and level of practice can vary with experience and individual circumstances. When individual guidance is needed, learn from a suitably qualified practitioner.

Begin gradually

Technique, comfort and appropriate guidance are more important than forcing duration or intensity.

Pranayama & Wellness

Potential Benefits of Pranayama

Pranayama can be incorporated into yoga, meditation and healthy-lifestyle routines. Potential benefits depend on the practice and how it is performed.

Breathing Awareness

Develop greater awareness of breathing patterns and conscious breath control.

Relaxation

Breathing practices may be included in relaxation and mindfulness routines.

Mindfulness

Conscious breathing can be incorporated into meditation and mindful practice.

Focused Practice

Traditional breathing practices are often used alongside yoga and meditation.

Practice safely

Pranayama is a complementary wellness practice and should not replace diagnosis, prescribed medication or medical treatment. Stop if you experience dizziness, discomfort or unusual symptoms.
