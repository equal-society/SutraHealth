Understanding Pranayama

What Is Pranayama?

Pranayama is a traditional yogic practice based on conscious awareness and regulation of the breath. Different pranayama breathing exercises use different patterns of inhalation, exhalation and breath control.

Breathing practices are traditionally included in yoga, meditation and mindful wellness routines. This page introduces eight practices and provides general educational information about their role in a yoga practice.

06

Sheetali – Sheetkari

Traditional cooling-oriented pranayama practices commonly incorporated into yoga routines.

07

Brahmari

Close ears with both thumbs, hear inner sound and produce humming vibration.

08

Omkar

A traditional practice combining conscious breathing with vocalization of the Om sound.

Pranayama for Beginners

How to Practice Pranayama

Beginners can start by learning one breathing technique at a time and focusing on comfortable, controlled breathing rather than forcing the breath or increasing intensity.

Practice in a comfortable position and follow appropriate instruction for the technique being learned. If you experience dizziness, discomfort or unusual symptoms, stop and return to comfortable normal breathing.

Individual suitability can vary. When guidance is needed, learn from a suitably qualified yoga or healthcare professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

Pranayama FAQs

What is pranayama?

Pranayama is a traditional yogic practice involving conscious awareness and regulation of the breath through different breathing techniques.

What are the main types of pranayama?

This page introduces Kapalbhati, Anulom Vilom, Naadi Shodhan, Bhastrika, Ujjayi, Sheetali-Sheetkari, Brahmari and Omkar.

Which pranayama is suitable for beginners?

Beginners should learn breathing techniques gradually and comfortably. The appropriate practice depends on experience, goals and individual circumstances.

Can pranayama be used for relaxation?

Controlled breathing practices are commonly included in relaxation, mindfulness and meditation routines. Practice gently and stop if you feel uncomfortable.

What precautions should be taken during pranayama?

Avoid forcing the breath. Stop if you experience dizziness, discomfort or unusual symptoms. Seek appropriate professional guidance when individual advice is needed.
